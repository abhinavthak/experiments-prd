0.Overview & stakeholders

At a glance

What
An experiments panel inside the University CMS. The growth team tests a page change (a section swap or a different URL) on a share of visitors, measures leads and enrolments, and ships the winner as the page default.
Test types
Two choices per test: what is compared (original vs variant on the same URL, or URL vs URL) and how traffic is split (fixed A/B test, or a multi-armed bandit that shifts traffic to the best variant) (3.1, 7).
Parts
Experiments list (4), create flow (5), experiment page (6), split URL tests (7) and supporting pages: Insights, Holdouts, Metrics, Audiences, Reports, Settings (8).
How to read
Section 3 holds the rules every part follows. Sections 4 to 8 cover one part each. Section 9 lists the tests that prove it works.
Built on
CMS pages and their section variants, the page editor, the lead form and CRM tagging, the analytics data layer, GrowthBook's experiment model and statistics.
Key risks
Teams calling a test early on too little data (3.4, 3.5). Two tests on one page contaminating each other (3.3). Redirect tests hurting SEO if canonicals are wrong (7).
Still to settle
Three facts decide most open cases: whether university sites sit behind a CDN, how a CRM lead links to its enrolment and payment, and the typical enquiry-to-payment lag (0.4, 9).

0.1Problem

Program pages are changed on opinion today. A new banner, CTA or fee block goes live for everyone, and nobody can say whether it brought more enquiries or fewer. Seasonal swings in traffic hide the effect of any single change.

Experiments fix this. A share of visitors sees the change, the rest see today's page, and the panel compares leads and enrolments between the two groups. The winner ships only when the data says so.

0.2Key terms

TermMeaning
ExperimentOne test on one page, with a name, tracking key, hypothesis, variants, targeting, metrics and a stop rule.
VariantA version of the page. A (Control) is what visitors see today. B to E are the changes. In a split URL test each variant is a URL.
Page defaultThe variant every visitor sees when no test is running. Shipping a winner makes it the page default.
Tracking keyA unique slug, e.g. online-bba-banner-cta, sent with every exposure and lead. Locked once the test starts.
RolloutThe share of eligible visitors who enter the test. The rest see the page default and are not counted.
Primary metricThe one number that decides the winner, e.g. Lead form submitted. A bandit calls it the decision metric.
GuardrailA metric that must not get worse by more than its cutoff, e.g. Bounce rate may rise at most 3%, page load time at most 5% (3.4).
LiftThe relative change against Control. A 10% lift on a 7.5% rate means 7.5% to 8.25%.
PhaseA period of a running test with fixed split, rollout and targeting. Changing those starts a new phase and restarts results.
Launch checklistAutomatic checks plus manual QA items that must pass before a test can start (3.2).
CallThe panel's recommendation for a running test: Ship now, Keep running, Review, Roll back, Not enough data or Unhealthy (3.4).

2.User stories

2.1Growth marketer

  • I want to try a shorter fee block on the Online BBA page on a quarter of visitors, so I don't risk the whole admissions season on a guess.
  • I want to know before starting whether a 10% lift in enquiries is even detectable on a page with 600 visitors a day.
  • I want to test a redesigned landing page built as a separate CMS page, without engineering setting up redirects.
  • I want enquiries submitted later on the apply page to count for the page that persuaded the visitor.

2.2Growth lead

  • I want no one to ship a winner on day 3 because it looked good, so early calls are blocked until the planned sample is reached.
  • I want a winner that slows the page by more than 5% held for review, even if enquiries went up.
  • I want every finished test to leave a written learning, so we stop re-testing ideas that already lost.

2.3Admissions and program teams

  • I want to know what visitors on my program page are seeing this week, before a counsellor gets a confused call.
  • I want enrolments and payments, not only clicks, to decide winners on fee-related tests.

2.4Engineering and SEO

  • I want variants rendered server-side and cached correctly, so pages don't flicker and stay fast.
  • I want crawlers always to see the original and redirected URLs to carry the right canonical, so rankings don't move.

Launch checklist. Start (now or scheduled) is blocked until every required item passes. Automatic checks: the page is live, a primary metric is set, there is at least one variant besides Control, the split adds up to 100%, rollout is above 0%, no other test is running or scheduled on the page, a bandit uses a conversion metric, and a hypothesis is written if settings require one. Manual items are set by an Admin, e.g. "Checked every variant on mobile and desktop". Optional items only warn.

3.3Who is in the test

  1. Eligible: the visitor matches the audience (all, new, returning), device (mobile, desktop or both) and every attribute condition.
  2. In the rollout: a hash of the assignment ID falls inside the rollout %. Others see the page default and are not counted.
  3. Assigned: the same hash picks the variant by the split. Sticky bucketing keeps the visitor there if the split changes later.
SettingRule
Attribute conditionsRows of attribute → operator → value, joined by AND. Attributes: country, city, browser, UTM source, medium and campaign, logged in, lead stage, page visits. Each row shows a plain sentence, e.g. "Only visitors whose country is IN".
Reach estimatePage traffic × rollout × device share × the share each condition keeps, from the site's traffic mix (India 82%, Chrome 68%, paid search 24%…).
Assignment IDVisitor ID (cookie) by default; lead ID or session ID, with a fallback if the first is missing.
Exclude existing leadsVisitors who already submitted a form on this page always see Control.
One test per pageOnly one running or scheduled test per page. A second can be drafted; the checklist blocks its Start.
Never countedCMS admins, preview links, bots and visitors with cookies blocked.

3.4Statistics and the call

Organization settings (8) set the defaults; a test can change them in step 5 before it starts.

SettingRule
EngineBayesian: chance to beat Control, risk and a credible interval; a winner is called at 95% chance to win. Frequentist: p-value and confidence interval; significant below the alpha. Optional sequential testing makes it safe to check results any time.
CUPEDUses visitors' pre-test behaviour to narrow intervals.
Many metricsHolm-Bonferroni or Benjamini-Hochberg correction across metrics and variants.
MinimumsMinimum run time (default 7 days) and minimum sample per metric before any call. Changes beyond a set size are flagged Suspicious.

Guardrail cutoffs

Every metric has a guardrail cutoff: the most it may get worse, relative to Control, when used as a guardrail. Default 2%; set per metric on the Metrics page (8). A small, real worsening below the cutoff does not block a winner.

StateWhenEffect
Within cutoffEven the worst plausible value (the bad end of the interval) is inside the cutoffNone
WatchNot clearly past the cutoff, but the interval reaches beyond itShown on Results and Health; does not block
FailingClearly worse than Control (significant) and the estimate is past the cutoffRed banner; the call becomes Review instead of Ship now when the setting is on
Not enough dataBelow the metric's minimum sampleNone yet

Example: Bounce rate, cutoff 3%. Variant B is 2.4% worse with an interval up to 6.8% worse: Watch. Variant C on page load is 37.6% slower, significantly: Failing.

CallWhen
UnhealthySample ratio mismatch (traffic split differs from the plan, χ² p < 0.001) or too many visitors who saw more than one variant
Not enough dataBefore the minimum sample per metric
EarlyLooks like a winner, but the minimum run time or the planned sample (3.5) isn't reached yet. Shows "x% of planned sample"
MaturingThe primary metric's conversion window hasn't passed for everyone exposed (payment, enrolment)

3.5How long a test needs

The plan answers one question before a test starts: how many days until it can reliably detect the smallest lift worth caring about. It uses 80% power, the organization's confidence level, and splits alpha across variants (alpha ÷ (variants − 1)).

Conversion metrics use the standard two-proportion sample size per variant, with p₂ = p₁ × (1 + lift) and p̄ their mean:

n = ( z1−α/2·√(2p̄(1−p̄)) + z1−β·√(p₁(1−p₁) + p₂(1−p₂)) )² ÷ (p₂ − p₁)²

Days = n × variants ÷ daily visitors entering the test. Before start, daily visitors and the Control rate are estimated (page traffic, rollout, targeting, minus any holdout share; the metric's usual rate). Once running, both are measured, and the plan shows days left instead.

Check
A 3% baseline and a 20% lift with 2 variants at 95% confidence needs 13,914 visitors per variant, the same as standard online calculators.

3.6Health checks

  • Sample ratio mismatch: actual split vs the planned split (for bandits, the day's weights), χ² test.
  • Multiple exposures: visitors who saw more than one variant, above the settings threshold.
  • Enough data: per metric, against the minimum sample.
  • Guardrails: each variant is Within cutoff, Watch or Failing per guardrail (3.4).
  • Mid-test edits: changes to variant sections while the test runs, listed with time and author.
  • Traffic by dimension: split by device, country and source, each with its own χ².

6.3Make changes

A running test can't be edited in place. Make changes offers two paths:

  • Increase rollout: raises the share of visitors in the test and keeps all data (same phase).
  • New phase: new split, rollout, audience or device, with a reason. Visitors are re-assigned and results restart; earlier phases stay selectable on Results.

6.4Stop

FieldRule
ResultWon, Lost, No clear winner, Did not finish. Pre-filled from the call.
Winning variantShown only for Won.
What visitors see afterOriginal vs variant: the chosen variant becomes the page default. Split URL: the redirect is removed, or becomes a permanent 301 to the winner (7).
ConclusionWhat happened, why, what to try next. Shown on Results and in Insights.

A test that hits its stop date stops by itself, shows everyone Control, and asks the owner to record a result.
